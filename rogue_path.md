# Rogue Path — Conceptual Design

## The two processes

A mage's channel degrades through two qualitatively distinct processes. Both accumulate over time, both end the character as they were, and both are available to any mage in the right circumstances. The channel doesn't distinguish intention.

**Consumption** is internal structural damage. The channel deteriorates from overload, losing efficiency, precision, and capacity. It's entropy. The endpoint is a destroyed channel; the mage stops being a mage.

**Poisoning** is external contamination. Energy extracted from living sources carries something the channel cannot process cleanly. The channel doesn't deteriorate, it changes. The endpoint is a lost self; the mage stops being themselves.

Both processes can run in the same character simultaneously.

---

## What draining a living source means

Draining a living source produces less usable energy than equivalent ambient extraction because the energy is dirtier. The clean fraction goes to the mage; the dirty fraction goes into the channel as contamination. The net yield is lower, and the contamination cost is paid regardless.

The source depletes visibly. Draining something withers or incinerates it, and anyone present sees it. The social and moral weight of that depends on context.

The barrier to using this technique is narrative, not mechanical. There is no lock, no prerequisite. What stops a mage from doing it is the same thing that stops a person from killing a stranger in line at the supermarket: it's wrong, it's visible, they're not that kind of person, not yet, not unless. The circumstances that make it feel necessary are the circumstances that make it a real choice.

---

## Source efficiency

Not all living sources produce meaningful contamination. The relevant factor is a rough combination of mass and cognitive complexity, and the two aren't independent.

Insects, grass, small organisms: negligible contamination, negligible yield. Not worth the act.

Animals of significant size or behavioral complexity: moderate yield, light contamination. A mage with a poor channel might prefer these because they're more reactive and accessible than large stable sources.

Large ancient plants, old-growth forests: high mass, low reactivity. Stable sources that may resist extraction from an underdeveloped channel, high yield when accessible.

People and cognitively complex creatures: highest yield, highest contamination, highest moral and social weight. The sources that cost most ethically pay most energetically and cost most to the channel. This is not a coincidence in the system's design.

The progression is natural: a mage in crisis reaches for what's available and what they can justify. Easier sources come first. The craving that develops with advanced poisoning pushes toward higher-yield sources.

---

## Symptoms

Consumption and poisoning share some early symptoms, which makes diagnosis difficult. They diverge as both processes advance.

Consumption manifests as progressive loss. The mage does less, controls less, reaches less. The channel narrows. Symptoms map to capability degradation.

Poisoning manifests as progressive transformation. The mage changes. Symptoms map to behavioral drift and perceptual distortion, not capability loss. At advanced stages the contaminated channel may access higher raw magnitude than a clean one, at the cost of all precision and at the cost of the person operating it.

One early symptom of poisoning is worth isolating: the mage begins to prefer dirty ether. The craving appears before any other obvious change, disguised as a practical preference. This is already "you are no longer yourself" expressed as a shift in taste rather than personality. It's the first symptom that doesn't read as ordinary stress or fatigue.

At advanced stages, the mage may become deliberately negligent about components. They stop maintaining supply. The absence of alternatives stops feeling like a problem.

---

## Components as moral infrastructure

A mage who maintains a good component supply reduces reliance on ambient extraction and avoids the choice entirely. The system doesn't state this as a rule. It's a conclusion you reach after understanding what the alternatives cost.

Arriving in a critical situation without components is not a resource management failure. It's a question about who you are and what you'll do next.

---

## Design principle

The physics records, it doesn't judge. A mage who drains a person to save ten people accumulates the same poisoning as one who does it for convenience. The channel processes the act, not the intention behind it. The moral weight lives in the fiction. The mechanics produce consequences that the fiction then has to carry.
